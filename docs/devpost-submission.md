# Devpost Submission Draft — DealFlow AI

## Project name

DealFlow AI

## Elevator pitch

AI agents negotiate the deal. Deterministic limits enforce the budget and the seller's private floor. Both humans approve. PayPal settles.

## Inspiration

Freelance and agency deals die in the boring middle: the back-and-forth over price, scope, and delivery. An agent can do that work well. But handing an agent the cheque book is a bad idea. We wanted to build the version that is actually safe enough to use: the AI does the negotiation, the limits are code, and the humans keep the irreversible decisions.

## What it does

A buyer creates a deal with a budget and delivery window. A seller submits an opening offer, a delivery window, and a private floor. Two AI agents negotiate:

- The buyer agent proposes and counters inside the budget.
- The seller agent responds inside the floor.
- The seller floor never enters the buyer agent's context or the buyer's UI.
- The agents produce an immutable agreement.
- Both humans must approve the same version. One rejection cancels the deal.
- PayPal checkout unlocks only after both approvals.
- The PayPal order is captured and verified; the capture ID and audit trail are stored.

The demo includes a paid sandbox deal with capture `84437967V6387225V`.

## How we built it

- **Laravel 13 + Inertia 3 + React 19** for the workspace.
- **Gemini 3 Flash** through the Laravel AI SDK for the buyer and seller proposals.
- **Deterministic validators** for the budget, floor, delivery window, approval state, and payment preconditions.
- **PayPal Orders API v2** for order creation and capture.
- **PayPal webhooks** with signature verification and idempotent event storage.
- **SQLite** for the demo, with an audit log for every state change.
- **PHPUnit** for negotiation, approval, rejection, checkout, capture, and webhook tests.

The AI is allowed to reason and propose. It is not allowed to approve terms, reveal the seller floor, or move money.

## Challenges

- Keeping the AI genuinely in charge of the negotiation while keeping the irreversible steps deterministic.
- Making the seller floor useful to the seller without leaking it into the buyer agent's context.
- Getting webhook signature verification right, including rejecting malformed deliveries without crashing.
- Making the approval gate impossible to bypass: the payment action checks the agreement state, not just the UI.

## Accomplishments

- A real agent-to-agent negotiation: **$400 → $580 → $500**, with the final buyer counter generated by Gemini.
- A complete human approval gate for both parties.
- A real PayPal sandbox capture, not a mocked success screen.
- A signed webhook path with idempotent event handling.
- An audit trail for proposals, approvals, payment events, and capture.

## What we learned

The interesting product is not the chat. It is the boundary. Users will trust an agent to negotiate when they can see exactly what it cannot do.

## What's next

- Seller-side notification preferences and email delivery.
- Refunds, disputes, and partial milestones.
- More negotiation policies: delivery penalties, revision limits, and multi-line scopes.
